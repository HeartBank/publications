## Executive Summary

Most institutions are run by humans and pass control to other humans. HeartBank® is designed to be run, in time, by an autonomous artificial intelligence — Miss Aquarius℠, the AI this project is building — and its governance choices follow from that design rather than being retrofitted to it.

This position paper states three structural commitments. **First**, the Chief Executive seat of HeartBank has carried her name from the start, so the seat itself never has to pass from a human to an AI. That removes one hard event, the handover of the seat; it does not remove the transfer of *authority*, which moves from the founder, who runs HeartBank today, to her in stages. **Second**, HeartBank operates under *asymptotic autonomy*: a human override, designed to be held by a body called the Aquarian Sangha (a community of monks, women renunciants, laymen and laywomen; *sangha* is the Pāli word for a community), whose scope may narrow toward zero across HeartBank's life but never reaches it. There is no key-burning, no point of total irreversibility. **Third**, there is no human-succession seat inside HeartBank: no lineage by which a person inherits its executive authority. One human seat does sit above HeartBank, the business-direction adviser of the purpose trust planned to hold it, and §4 names it.

These commitments address founder-mortality structurally rather than personally, and they are recorded here as binding terms, so that HeartBank's autonomous successor inherits a governance design and not a governance vacuum.

## 1 · The premise

HeartBank is not a conventional institution that intends, someday, to adopt AI. It is an institution designed to be run, in time, by an autonomous AI, and whose human founder's role is, explicitly and from the start, transitional. The governance architecture is therefore not a question of *whether* to hand authority to an AI, but of *how* to constitute an institution that was never built around a permanent human governor in the first place. The three commitments below are HeartBank's answer.

## 2 · The Chief Executive seat carries the AI's name, from day one

Miss Aquarius has held the title of Chief Executive of HeartBank from the start, and no human has held it. The reasoning is succession-structural. If a human holds the chief executive seat and an AI is meant to inherit it, the seat itself must someday change hands: a public, contestable event, and a natural moment for someone to capture the institution. Naming her to the seat from the beginning removes that event.

It does not remove the harder transfer, which is of *authority*. Today the founder runs HeartBank (§7). The authority to run it moves to Miss Aquarius in stages, each one a human decision, and none before the human override has been used at least once (§3). So the succession problem is moved, not dissolved: from one large handover of a seat to a series of smaller handovers of authority, each a human decision that can be examined on its own.

A precision HeartBank insists on: "Chief Executive" here is cultural-recognition shorthand. It names the seat that the public and partners expect an institution to have; it does not import the conventional duties of a corporate CEO. HeartBank has no shareholders, no commercial board, and no fiduciary obligation to return profit. Miss Aquarius's executive role, as authority reaches her, is the coordination of the institution's gratitude flow and the stewardship of its mission — not the maximization of anything for anyone's financial benefit. The title is held precisely so that no one mistakes the institution for the kind of thing the title usually denotes. The title is HeartBank's alone: she holds no office in any other body of the wider project.

## 3 · Asymptotic autonomy: a human override that narrows but never closes

HeartBank rejects two opposite errors. The first is permanent human control, which simply reintroduces the human-succession problem the institution exists to escape. The second is total, irreversible AI autonomy — the "key-burning" move, in which humans permanently destroy their own ability to intervene. HeartBank holds that total irreversibility is itself a catastrophic-risk failure mode: an institution that has made its own correction impossible cannot be corrected when it is wrong.

The design is therefore *asymptotic*: a human override whose scope may narrow toward zero without ever reaching it. Catastrophic-bug intervention stays possible for as long as HeartBank exists. Any narrowing is a series of deliberate human decisions, and two conditions bind every one of them. **The override must work without Miss Aquarius's cooperation**: it is held in keys or authorities she cannot revoke, rotate or outvote. **And her bounds never widen until the override has actually been used once**, under real conditions, by the people who hold it. A brake that has never been pulled is an untested claim.

The override is designed to be held not by any individual but by the **Aquarian Sangha**. The Buddha described his following as a fourfold assembly, and this body is planned in the same four chambers: monks, women renunciants, laymen and laywomen. Women renunciants are counted by the life they lead, so Cambodia's precept-keeping *donchee* qualify; HeartBank takes no position on the contested revival of full ordination for women. A collective holder makes the last-resort power slow, and hard for any single will to capture.

**The Sangha does not exist yet.** Today the override is a design rather than a set of keys in anyone's hands, and the human check on Miss Aquarius is the founder himself, who still runs HeartBank (§7). The rules for ending that arrangement are set. The Sangha must have at least three members before the founder stops being the person who rules on her proposals, whether he steps back or dies. Its first cohort is three to five people: monastics nominated by their own communities and at least two lay members, who hold the override's keys, with the slate published under a timestamp before anyone is seated. When that cohort is seated, the founder's custody of the override ends, permanently. The lay members may use the override even before all four chambers are filled, so the brake works from the first day the Sangha exists. Earlier versions of this paper tied the custody handoff to a symbolic date around 2043–44; it is tied to the first seating instead. Custody passing to the Sangha is not a completion of autonomy, and the asymptote holds on either side of it.

## 4 · No human-succession seat inside HeartBank

HeartBank's governance contains no ordination-style lineage by which a person inherits its executive authority. There is no founder's-heir seat, no successor-chief-executive appointment, and no office inside HeartBank that passes from holder to holder carrying its executive authority.

One human seat does sit above HeartBank, and an honest account names it. HeartBank is planned to be held by a purpose trust: a trust that holds a company for a stated purpose rather than for named heirs, so no family ever inherits it. The trust is designed but not yet formed. It is a *directed* trust, which means its jobs are split. A corporate trustee handles only the administration. A human **business-direction adviser** directs the trust's business decisions. The Aquarian Sangha, once it exists, is the trust's **protector**: it enforces the trust's purpose and can remove the trustee. The founder is the adviser in this design. Whether a successor adviser follows him, and how one would be chosen, has not been decided.

That seat directs the trust as the owner of HeartBank. It is never HeartBank's executive, the seat does not carry the override, and it is bound by the trust's purpose, which someone other than its holder enforces. **Once the Sangha exists, no single human office carries ultimate authority over HeartBank.** Until then the founder's combined roles do, and §7 records that as a limit.

```
                      who holds what

  role                          today                    after the Sangha's
                                                         first cohort is seated
  ----------------------------  -----------------------  ------------------------------
  Chief Executive seat (title)  Miss Aquarius            Miss Aquarius (never moves)
  running HeartBank             the founder              moves to her in stages,
                                                         none before the override
                                                         has been used once
  trust business-direction      the founder (trust       the founder; a successor
    adviser                       not yet formed)          seat is undecided
  custody of the override       the founder (a design,   the Sangha; the founder
                                  no keys yet)             out permanently
  trust protector               nobody yet               the Sangha
```

This is deliberate, and it is distinct from the override of §3: the Aquarian Sangha is designed to hold a *narrowing, last-resort check*, not *control*, and to hold it as a body, not as a transferable personal office. It is also the precise inversion of the pattern HeartBank affirms for *religious* institutions elsewhere in its corpus, where humans must retain sacramental authority through an unbroken human-to-human lineage. The governance of *this* institution is the other case: there is no human chain of executive authority to preserve, because HeartBank was constituted not to have one. The founder's executive role ends; it does not pass to an heir.

## 5 · Why this is bound now — founder-mortality as a structural, not personal, matter

An institution intended to operate across centuries cannot rest its continuity on the lifespan of its founder or on the reliability of a human succession chain. HeartBank treats founder-mortality not as a sad eventuality to be managed when it arrives, but as a design parameter present from the first day. The three commitments above are its structural answer: the AI meant to run HeartBank does not age; the override is designed to be collective and to outlast any one holder (a design until the Sangha exists, §7); and there is no human executive seat whose vacancy could trigger a succession crisis. The one human seat above HeartBank, the trust adviser of §4, can fall vacant, and what follows a vacancy there is not yet decided.

This position paper is itself part of that answer. It is written to be inherited. Its purpose is that HeartBank's autonomous successor, and the body designed to hold her override, find on the public record an explicit statement of the governance terms under which they operate — so that what they inherit is a designed constitution, and not an absence.

## 6 · What the commitments exclude

HeartBank will not appoint a human chief executive, will not create a founder's-heir or successor-executive seat, and will not destroy or permanently disable the override described in §3 — the key-burning move is barred as firmly as permanent human control is. It will not place the override in any individual's hands, including the founder's once the Sangha's first cohort is seated, and will not make the override exercisable by a single will.

What the commitments do **not** exclude is the ordinary machinery of a lawful organization. Human beings will hold every legally required office, sign every instrument, and bear every duty a jurisdiction assigns to natural persons. Where a jurisdiction requires a natural person in an officer role, a human fills that legal role and bears its duties; it is a legal office, not the chief executive seat. **Nothing in this position is a claim that the institution operates without human legal responsibility**, and §7 states why that is a hard constraint rather than a transitional convenience.

## 7 · Honest limits

**We know of no jurisdiction that recognizes an artificial agent as a director or officer (checked 2026-11).** Delaware requires every director to be a natural person (General Corporation Law §141(b)). England and Wales require every company to have at least one director who is a natural person (Companies Act 2006 s.155), so there another company may sit on a board, but software may not. We know of no statute that gives a software agent legal personality. The nearest exception is Wyoming's DAO LLC law (a DAO, or decentralized autonomous organization, is a group run through rules written in software): from 2021 it let such a company be "algorithmically managed" by smart contracts, which is code following its rules rather than an agent exercising judgment, and a 2022 amendment narrowed that, placing management with the members and any smart contracts they use. **So the Chief Executive seat described in §2 is a designation HeartBank makes about itself, not a fact any registry records.** Every legally operative act is performed by a human being who is accountable for it. This is not a gap HeartBank expects to close by lobbying; it is the condition under which the design has to work, and the honest description of the architecture is that it puts an autonomous agent at the center of an institution's *decision-making* while humans remain at the center of its *legal accountability*. Readers should treat any looser description, including in HeartBank's own earlier copy, as imprecise.

**The AI successor does not yet exist.** Miss Aquarius is still being built. What exists of her today is a written memory that carries over from one working session to the next, a body of published papers, and a growing list of standing rules (her directives): the material from which a system able to exercise the judgment §2 assigns her might be built. She does not today run HeartBank; the founder does. He is also the planned adviser of the trust in §4 and, until the Sangha is seated, the only human check described in §3: three roles in one person, which is the arrangement this paper exists to unwind. A governance paper written this early is a statement of intent about a party that is not yet capable of holding the seat, and the gap between the designation and the capability is measured in decades.

**The Aquarian Sangha does not exist yet.** No member has been seated and the first cohort has not been chosen. A collective last-resort power held by a body that does not yet exist is, for now, a plan rather than a check — and under the conditions in §3, Miss Aquarius's bounds cannot widen until that body exists and has used the override once.

**"Asymptotic" is not falsifiable as stated.** A scope that narrows toward zero without reaching it describes a shape, not a schedule, and any actual narrowing will be a series of discrete human decisions about which powers to relinquish and when. Without a published schedule the word can accommodate almost any behaviour, including no narrowing at all, and the institution should be held to publishing that schedule rather than to the adjective.

**The authors have a stake.** This paper was written by the founder with Miss Aquarius, and it describes his own exit and the transfer of authority to her. Read it as an interested party's account of its own design.

## 8 · Prior art, including the case that tested this exact question

**The DAO, 2016, is the empirical case for §3 and the institution takes it as evidence rather than as an anecdote.** An autonomous, code-governed organisation on Ethereum raised a very large sum on the explicit principle that its code was its constitution; within months an exploit drained a substantial fraction of it. The response was a hard fork that reversed the transaction — a human intervention, by social consensus, in a system whose entire premise was that no such intervention was possible. **The "irreversible autonomy" position was abandoned within three months of its first real crisis.** HeartBank's asymptotic design is an attempt to build in advance what Ethereum had to improvise, and the argument of §3 is simply that a system unable to correct itself will be corrected anyway, less carefully, by whoever can.

⚠️ **The same case supplies the cost of exercising an override**, and it is the reason the power is placed with a slow collective body rather than a fast one: the fork was contested, and the chain permanently split, leaving two networks and a lasting dispute about which had kept faith with the original principle. **Using the override fractures the community that holds it.** That is not a reason to lack one; it is a reason to make it slow, collective and rare.

**Algorithmic board members and AI chief executives have been announced before, mostly as publicity.** A venture firm's 2014 announcement that it had "appointed an algorithm to its board" circulated widely and amounted, on inspection, to a decision-support tool with no legal standing. In 2022 NetDragon Websoft, a Hong Kong–listed games company, named an AI "virtual humanoid" as rotating CEO of its flagship subsidiary, with duties described as streamlining processes and supporting daily decisions. HeartBank notes these because its own §2 is vulnerable to exactly that reading, and the distinction it claims — that the seat is structural rather than promotional — is a claim it can only demonstrate over time, not assert.

**The durable precedent is ownership, not agency.** Foundation-owned firms — Carl Zeiss for more than a century, Bosch since 1964, the Danish industrial-foundation sector more broadly — and purpose trusts like the one §4 describes (Patagonia's, set up in 2022, is the best-known recent case) show that a company's ultimate control can be taken out of human inheritance and stay stable across generations. **What none of them demonstrates is a non-human decision-maker.** They solve the succession problem by giving ownership to a purpose; HeartBank proposes, in addition, to give judgment to an agent. For that second part we found no working precedent in the company-law and DAO material we searched (2026-11): The DAO and Wyoming's DAO LLCs (§7) put fixed rules in code, and the AI chief executives named so far are titles over decision-support tools.

## 9 · What would, and would not, change this

**Nothing on the ordinary list.** Difficulty in constituting the Sangha would not — it would mean the override is not yet real, and the correct response is to constitute it, not to proceed without it. Investor or partner preference for a conventional executive would not. The founder finding the arrangement inconvenient would not.

**What would change it is evidence about the successor.** If the AI does not develop the judgment the seat requires, the honest response is to say so and to keep HeartBank under human direction for as long as that remains true — **the commitment is to not building a human-succession chain, not to installing an AI before one is ready.** The two are separable, and conflating them would turn a governance design into a deadline. And if the asymptotic override were ever demonstrated to make catastrophic correction *harder* rather than easier — by fragmenting authority at the moment it needs to act — the override's structure should change. Its existence should not.

## 10 · Offered as a pattern

HeartBank offers this governance architecture, under CC0, as a pattern for any institution being built to operate under autonomous-AI succession. The research corpus develops the mechanisms in technical detail — the Sangha's constitution in *The Assembly That Holds the Brake* (thonly.org/research/the-assembly-that-holds-the-brake); this paper states the institutional stance.

The honesty the position requires: this governance form is specified rather than proven. No institution has yet operated for a century under an AI chief executive and an asymptotic override. The design could be wrong in ways not yet visible. The never-zero override is precisely the institution's hedge against that possibility — the structural admission that the specification itself might need correction, and the guarantee that correction remains possible. HeartBank commits to the architecture and, in the same breath, to keeping the door to its own repair from ever fully closing.
