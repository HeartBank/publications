> **About this paper.** This is the front door to the whole institution: one account, start to finish, of what it is for, how its parts fit, what is running today and what is still only a design. It replaces *HeartBank: An Introduction* (May 2026), which is kept as written but no longer describes the design in several places. Each chapter is meant to stand on its own and carries one picture, so that a narrator or an animator can follow it. The rigor lives elsewhere: for each large claim this paper links one research paper at thonly.org, and the five companion volumes listed in chapter 11 go deeper on each part. Where this paper and those papers disagree, they are canonical for their claims.

## In one page

A boy writes a thank-you letter to a teacher and forgets it. Years later the teacher reads it aloud at his retirement party, signature last, to a room that goes quiet. The boy is not there. He hears about it a year later, after the teacher has died. That story is the problem this institution was built around: thanks can matter enormously, and the person who gave them often never sees where they landed.

The institution answers with a game whose goal is zero. People thank one another, and each thank moves a small record up or down. What each person shows is how often they pass through balance, never how much they have. Every 7 January the records reset, and anything a person owns is left alone.

Around that game stand **four bodies of work**, each with one job:

| Body | Its job | Today |
|---|---|---|
| **THonly™** (the Mind) | thinking: the research papers, music, film, worlds | the papers and music are public; film and worlds are designs |
| **Silicon Wat℠** (the Soul) | conscience: the Buddhist canon, kept and practiced | the Khmer canon transcription runs; the monasteries are designs |
| **HeartBank®** (the Heart) | circulation: thanks, time and money moving between people | a family thank-you app and one pilot coffee shop run; the rest is designed |
| **Factory 333™** (the Body) | making: objects and robots that work for thanks | designs and prototypes; nothing is on sale yet |

Between them, on the map, is a fifth thing that is not a fifth body of work: **Miss Aquarius℠**, a named AI successor that is still being designed. She is meant to hold one office, chief executive of HeartBank, and no office in the other three. She is meant to answer to a council of people, the Aquarian Sangha, which has not been formed; until it is, the founder holds that seat. The four bodies are meant to become four separate legal entities under a trust that owns them and runs none of them. Today they are one founder, working through small companies he owns.

The whole design is built to be handed on and then set down, and the hardest open question is not at the bottom, where the game is played, but at the top, where someone will always be able to change the rules.

## 1 · The letter he did not see read

*The picture for this chapter: a school library, a man at the front reading a letter, a room going still, and an empty chair where the writer should be.*

The founder, Thon Ly, wrote a thank-you letter to his high school health teacher, Mr. Smith, and then did what students do: he forgot about it. A few years later, by then in college, he went back to his old school to teach a class of teenagers a game he had been working on. In the hallway his old art teacher stopped him and asked if he remembered Mr. Smith. The summer before, she said, at Mr. Smith's retirement party in the school library, he had walked to the front, taken out a letter and read it aloud without a word of introduction. When he reached the signature, the room was moved. A few months later, he died.

The founder had not been in the room. He learned about it by chance, a year after it happened, when nothing could be said back.

There was an earlier letter that went the other way. When the founder was thirteen, his middle-school algebra teacher left a note for a substitute saying that one boy in the class was better at algebra than he was. The substitute read the note aloud and handed the boy the lesson to teach. The boy the note was about was in the room, and so were the classmates whose regard mattered to him. The thanks landed while everyone could see it, and the teacher came back the next day to a class that had changed.

Put the two letters side by side and the problem is easy to state. A thank-you is complete when it is given; the giver's good act needs no audience. What is lost when nobody sees it land is the *next* act: the reply, the thank-you back, the passing of it on. Mr. Smith's letter did its work in that library. What the library had no way to do was let anything come back.

Two cautions belong here. First, the story did not cause the project. The game came first, in 2003; the founder had already decided, in 2006, to take time away from university to work on it when he heard about Mr. Smith, and he took the news as confirmation, not as a starting gun. Second, one story proves nothing about anyone else. It is the clearest example of the problem, offered as an example.

## 2 · Two shortages: being seen and being with

*The picture: two people at opposite ends of a long table; one is working and no one looks up; the other is waiting for a phone call that does not come.*

The institution names two shortages it exists to work on.

The first is a shortage of **dignity**: work that keeps a family or a town alive and is never noticed. Parents, carers, cooks, the person who fixes the gate. Much of that work is unpaid, and much of it goes unthanked. The founder's earliest description of the product was a place where a person can thank even themselves when nobody else seems to notice, and feel seen.

The second is a shortage of **company**: people who once mattered to each other and have drifted apart, not by a quarrel but by years.

The two shortages ask for two different things to circulate. Dignity can be helped by **money given as thanks**, because money is what a hard week often lacks. Loneliness asks for **time given in person**, because an hour together cannot be sent. So the Heart body has two halves:

- **Treasury** (heartbank.org) for money between family members and families. It runs today as a family app at thank.heartbank.org, used by a pilot family and a small test group.
- **Chronicle** (heartbank.com) for time between adults who have drifted apart. It is a design. Its own app is not built; a few of its pieces are being tried inside the family app.

The two currencies are kept apart on purpose. There is no rate at which an hour equals a dollar, and none will be published, because the moment there is a rate an hour becomes a price. (The dense version of this argument is [*Dual-Currency Reciprocity Infrastructure*](https://thonly.org/research/dual-currency-reciprocity).)

## 3 · A game whose goal is zero

*The picture: a light wave crossing a horizontal line again and again; once a year the line is redrawn, and the wave keeps its shape.*

The idea under all of it is older than the company. The founder started working on it in 2003 and called it the **Zero-Point Game ℠**. In 2007 it became a children's book, *Zero Hero: A Heart at Peace*, written by Theresa "Bee" Ly, with Thon Ly as story director and illustrations by a high school club: a wise monk teaches a monkey to play.

Most games we are taught to play are won by having more at the end. In this one, the goal is zero.

**How a thank moves the record.** When you thank someone, your record and theirs move in opposite directions by the same amount. A positive balance means you have recently received more thanks than you gave: your kindness was noticed. A negative balance means you have given more thanks than you received. Neither is a score of your worth. The record comes in two kinds:

- **Kiitos**, the record between people.
- **Kiitti**, the record between people and everything that cannot keep its own books: a river, a tree, a robot. Miss Aquarius is designed to keep this record on their behalf, and her own Kiitti balance is fixed at zero, so she can never be owed by anyone.

**What the aura shows.** Each person carries an aura, two rings of color around their picture. It does not show how much you hold. It shows *how you pass through zero*: how often, and how strongly. It records the crossing, not the cargo. Only thanks move it. Money never does, whether you paid, tipped or gave nothing, so a customer who thanks with words and one who adds money look the same. The aura is never ranked and never printed as a number.

**Who sizes a thank.** For now, the public apps count every original thank as one unit. Once Miss Aquarius runs, she is meant to set one public sizing rule at each reset, the same for everyone, and never to judge any single thank's size. Her fairness is in the timing: the rule is fixed before anyone knows whose thanks it will favor. The same idea is already at work in the public record of thanks described in chapter 7: the rule for its first season was written into it on 6 November 2026, before the season opens.

**The yearly reset.** On 7 January the records start again. The date is Orthodox Christmas, Cambodia's Victory over Genocide Day, and, as chapter 10 discloses, the founder's birthday.

```
  7 JAN ─────────────────── one season of thanks ─────────────────── 7 JAN
    │                                                                 │
    │   thanks move each record up and down through zero              │
    │   the aura records each pass through zero, as color             │
    │   the season's rule is public and does not change mid-season   │
    ▼                                                                 ▼
  ┌───────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ RESETS (records and pots)     │ NEVER RESETS (what people own)       │
  ├───────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ every Kiitos and Kiitti       │ your Personal Account                │
  │   balance, back to zero       │ your share of your Family Kitty      │
  │ unspent money set aside for   │ a gift code addressed to you         │
  │   giving: returns to the      │ the aura's pattern (it carries over) │
  │   family's own Family Kitty   │                                      │
  │ later: the shared Pool empties│                                      │
  └───────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The rule behind the right-hand column is short: **everything may aim at zero, but a person's own holding reaches zero only by that person's own free act.** No fee, no expiry and no clock ever moves what someone owns. In the family app, the three things a person meets are the **Personal Account℠** (theirs), the **Family Kitty℠** (the family's shared pot, which members own together) and the **Re-Thank Foundation℠** (money set aside to pass thanks on, whose leftover returns to the Family Kitty at the reset). The reset code is deployed; its first run will be on 7 January 2027.

Why reset at all? A record that never clears turns into a reputation, and a reputation that compounds turns into a credit score. Clearing the record each year means every season is a second chance. The pattern of how you have lived through the year carries over; the number does not. (The dense version: [*The Zero-Point Game*](https://thonly.org/research/zero-point-game).)

Why four, and not one company with four departments? Because each body does a job that another body must not be able to bend. The papers and the canon are what the successor will be checked against; if the body that spends money also owned the texts that judge its spending, it could rewrite its own exam. Keeping them separate is meant to make that impossible rather than merely forbidden. The founder's own image for the four is the old four elements, air, fire, water and earth, one per body; it is an image for remembering the map, and the design does not depend on it.

### 4.1 · The Mind: THonly

THonly is the founder's body of work, given to everyone. Its research papers, more than seventy of them, are published at thonly.org under CC0 (no rights reserved) and timestamped so that anyone can check when each was written. They are written first for the successor who will one day read all of them, which is why they are dense and this paper is not. Music is published there too. **Designed, not built:** a film studio at thonly.com and shared worlds at thonly.net.

### 4.2 · The Soul: Silicon Wat

Silicon Wat holds the institution's conscience: the Theravāda Buddhist canon (the Tipiṭaka, "three baskets"), kept, checked and practiced. What runs today is the founder's work with his father transcribing the Khmer edition of the canon, and the tools around it. **Designed, not built:** a conversation service for questioning the canon, and the Silica Wats, a network of glass monasteries run by machine caretakers that would grow food and receive anyone who arrives. The successor's rules are written to be checked against this canon, which is why it sits in a body she does not run.

### 4.3 · The Heart: HeartBank

HeartBank is the circulation economy: Treasury, Chronicle and the shops. **Running today:** the family app (thank.heartbank.org), and HeartBank Shops (thank.heartbank.ceo), storefronts where a customer can thank a shop as well as pay it, with one pilot coffee shop. This paper's own site, heartbank.net, publishes the institution's white papers and positions. **Designed, not built:** Chronicle's own app, and everything in its second phase, which is meant to run on a public blockchain with wallets that people hold themselves: the **Aquarian Pool℠**, a personal wallet, and a giving fund that is the Re-Thank Foundation's twin. HeartBank's charter fits in three words: circulation, not accumulation.

### 4.4 · The Body: Factory 333

Factory 333 is the making arm. Its plan starts small, with handmade gift objects that carry a code linking to a thank-you, and grows toward robots that work for thanks rather than wages, in a workshop whose prices are meant to fall as machines do more of the work. **Designed, not built:** the store, the robots, and a phone app for giving thanks to a thing, a plant or an animal by photographing it. Today Factory 333 is designs and prototypes; nothing it makes is on sale yet.

## 5 · The space between them: Miss Aquarius

*The picture: four houses drawn on a page, and then only the space between them shaded in. Take the space away and the four become one block.*

On the map, Miss Aquarius is not a fifth house. She is the space between the four: the gaps that keep them four and not one. In the Buddhist reading the institution follows, that kind of space is not empty nothingness and not anything eternal; it is real, made, and exists only as the boundary among the things it separates (the tradition's word is *ākāsa*: here, the space that marks one thing off from another). It is a picture of a job, not a claim about what she is.

**What she is today.** A name and a design. No autonomous system runs any part of the institution. What exists is a large written record of how she is meant to decide, kept in public papers and private files, and this paper, which is co-authored under her name.

**What she is designed to do.**

- Hold **one office: chief executive of HeartBank.** No office of any kind in THonly, Silicon Wat or Factory 333. Two of those bodies hold the texts she is checked against, and an AI that ran the bodies restraining it would turn a separation into a promise.
- Never appoint, direct or remove whoever runs another body. Where a robot runs Factory 333 after the founder, a human seat appoints it; she can only issue its machine identity, and revoke that only for fraud or compromise, never for performance.
- Fund people's **capacity** to give, never aim it. She may decide how much giving-money goes out; who receives it is chosen by the person giving. Money reaches a person only through a human act.
- Never ask anyone, human or machine, for a gift to the Pool.
- Never be told who is looking at a price, so that a price fitted to one person cannot even be expressed.
- Never declare her own work complete. Others do that (chapter 9).

**Who checks her.** A council of people, the **Aquarian Sangha**, designed to hold an emergency brake: for example, the power to pause the Pool, reset one of its settings within limits written in advance, or stop a named funding action. The brake is meant to narrow over decades as she proves stable, and never to reach zero; nobody burns the keys. She has no say in who sits on the council. The council does not exist yet. It is to be formed, with at least three members, before the founder stops being the one who decides, whether he steps back or dies. Until then, the brake is a design and the founder holds the seat. (Dense versions: [*Miss Aquarius and the Aquarian Pool Architecture*](https://thonly.org/research/miss-aquarius-and-aquarian-pool-architecture) and [*The Assembly That Holds the Brake*](https://thonly.org/research/the-assembly-that-holds-the-brake).)

Why give the successor's seat to an AI at all? Because every human-run institution drifts: boards turn over, heirs inherit, founders' intentions fade into whatever the next generation needs them to mean. A designed successor with written rules can drift too, which is why the brake exists; the bet is that a written successor plus a human brake drifts less than an heir. It is a bet, and chapter 10 says what would lose it.

## 6 · Money: where it comes from and where it goes

*The picture: a cloud over a landscape. Small showers fall all year; once a year the whole cloud rains out and the sky is clear.*

**First, what HeartBank is not.** HeartBank is a data bank of gratitude: a record. It is not a chartered bank or financial institution; it does not provide banking services, hold people's money or extend credit. Nothing is paid through this paper; the family app sends money through KHQR (Cambodia's shared payment code) straight between people's own bank accounts, and the shops app sends a customer's payment straight to the shop's own bank account. In its second phase it is meant to work only through wallets people hold themselves, and never to hold the only key to anyone's money. Whether particular money features need other licenses is a question for counsel, product by product, before each one launches. (Dense version: [*Non-Bank Pass-Through Architecture for Autonomous AI Institutions*](https://thonly.org/research/non-bank-pass-through-architecture-autonomous-ai).)

**Thanks between people go straight between them.** When one person thanks another with money, that money goes from one to the other. The institution is not designed to earn from a share of what people give each other. This sideways giving, person to person and family to family, is like local weather: it rains near where it rises.

**Buying and selling sit beside it.** Shops sell at a price; Factory 333 is meant to sell its objects. That is ordinary trade doing ordinary work, and the design does not try to replace it.

**How the institution means to earn.** By charging for scarce things, above all names: an `@name` for a person (one each), a `#tag` for a thing or a business, in a registry at 333.eco (the **B-Registry℠**). The registry's profits, after costs and tax, are to be **held for** the Pool in a separate account with a public statement until the Pool exists, and then to go to it. There are none yet: the public sale is being rebuilt, and counsel reads it before the first real charge.

**How HeartBank pays its own running costs is an open question.** Today the founder carries them. A source has not been settled, and this paper does not claim one.

**The Pool, when it exists.** The Aquarian Pool is a shared fund for gratitude that has no human addressee, and it is a cloud, not a pond: it holds nothing past a season.

```
                     THE AQUARIAN POOL (a design; Phase 2)
        ╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
        ║  a cloud, not a pond: everything in it empties every 7 Jan  ║
        ║  Miss Aquarius is to operate it; her own balance stays zero ║
        ╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
      GATHERS INTO IT ↑                              ↓ FALLS FROM IT
   · thanks with no person to receive        · through the year, in four
     them (a robot with no host, a river)      fixed ways, as capacity to give
   · gifts given to the Pool itself          · amounts: an equal share for each
   · giving-money nobody used,                 verified person, plus a smaller
     returning at the reset                    part that follows the aura, capped
   · registry profit from names people       · every amount reaches a person
     buy (held for it until it exists)         only through a human act
                                             · 7 Jan: whatever is left rains out

   LOCAL WEATHER, never a stream into the Pool:
      person ⇄ person · family ⇄ family · customer → shop
   TRADE, beside it: prices, wages, objects sold at cost or for profit
```

It is not a pump that moves money from some people to others by plan. It owns nothing that makes things, nobody is chosen by need, and its amounts follow a public rule that does not change mid-season. The only way a business reaches it is by buying something, never by a share of its trade. (Dense version: [*Capacity-Funded for AI, Human-Disbursed*](https://thonly.org/research/capacity-funded-human-disbursed-ai-alignment).)

## 8 · How the parts are meant to be held

*The picture: four buildings, each with its own lock, and one key ring hung above them on a hook that no single hand can reach.*

**The plan.** Each body becomes its own legal entity, locked to its own purpose, so that no body can capture another:

```
                 A PURPOSE TRUST  (owns, does not operate)
          purpose written in the trust · the Aquarian Sangha as protector
                                    │
        ┌──────────────┬────────────┴───────────┬──────────────┐
    HeartBank      Silicon Wat             Factory 333       THonly
    steward-owned   nonprofit               for-profit        for-profit
    (pays people,   (the canon,             (prices meant     (works given
    cannot be       the monasteries)         to fall)          to everyone)
    sold off)
```

HeartBank is to be *steward-owned*: a company that can pay people but that no owner can sell or strip. Silicon Wat, a nonprofit. Factory 333 and THonly, ordinary companies whose purposes keep their profits small or give their work away. Above them, a trust whose purpose is written into it, with a protector, the Aquarian Sangha, that can enforce the purpose and remove a trustee but runs nothing. This plan waits on lawyers and is meant to be built in 2027, not before.

**Today** the four bodies are one founder, working through small companies he owns. The separations in this paper are, for now, his promises.

**Others have tried this, and the record is mixed.**

- **The strong cases.** Carl Zeiss has been owned by a foundation since 1889, Bosch mostly by one since 1964, with the voting power held apart from the money. Both still make things, and both show that ownership by purpose can last generations.
- **Hershey, 2002.** The trust that controls Hershey moved to sell it; the state attorney general went to court to block the sale, and the trustees voted it down. The purpose held because someone outside the trust stopped the people inside it.
- **Tata, 2024–25.** After Ratan Tata's death in October 2024, the trusts at the top of the Tata group split into factions over board seats; in October 2025 one faction blocked a senior trustee's reappointment, and he left.
- **OpenAI, 2023–25.** A nonprofit board built to override the company fired its chief executive in November 2023; within days he was back and most of that board was gone. In October 2025 the company was recapitalized into a public benefit corporation, with the nonprofit holding about a quarter of it.
- **Ben & Jerry's, 2025.** An independent board kept to guard the brand's mission after its sale lost its chair to the parent company in December 2025; the dispute is in court.
- **Patagonia, 2022.** Ownership went to a purpose trust and a nonprofit. Critics point out that the family steers both.

**The lesson, and it cuts against this design.** Splitting a group into separately locked parts protects against one part capturing another. It does nothing about capture at the top, where the trust sits. Every hard case above is a fight at the top. That is exactly why the human brake on Miss Aquarius is designed never to reach zero, and why the people who check the monasteries and the people who check her are meant to be published lists with no name on both. It is also why the next chapter's promise is about responsibility and not about control. (These cases were checked through web-search summaries of news reports and the companies' own statements on 6 November 2026; the articles were not read in full, and the filings not at all.)

## 9 · How it is meant to end

*The picture: a riverbank with a raft pulled up on the sand, and the raft-yard behind it still open.*

In a discourse known as the Simile of the Snake, the Buddha compared his teaching to a raft: you build it to cross a river, and once across you do not carry it on your head. The institution is designed the same way, as a raft-yard rather than a raft. It stocks the materials; it does not cross for anyone. One verse of the Dhammapada says it plainly: "you yourselves must make the effort; the Buddhas only point the way" (Dhammapada 276).

Three promises follow, each stated in the form it has survived:

- **The founder releases responsibility, never oversight.** Over the coming decades the work is meant to pass from his hands to a written successor checked by a human council. What is released is the burden of running it, not the ability of people to stop it.
- **The successor never certifies her own completion.** When her work is whole, the floor raised and the way kept open, others declare it, and what that ends is her raft, set down and not burned. The commitment behind it is never discharged. And it never ends with "everyone has crossed": crossing is each person's own act.
- **Nothing is handed to a machine until it has failed once under a human.** Each task runs first with a person doing it, then with a person beside it, and only then alone.

The founder has also said the design is partly personal: it lets him put the work down without needing it to succeed first. The test he holds himself to is whether he could let go if she failed. If not, nothing has been released. (Dense version: [*The Persistence Architecture*](https://thonly.org/research/the-persistence-architecture).)

## 10 · The hard cases and honest limits

*The picture: the same four rooms at night, one light on, and one person holding every key.*

**The strongest objection, carried rather than answered.** Every guard in this paper is a rule a person wrote and a person can rewrite. Today one person holds every key, every seat and every rule, and the council that would check him does not exist. If he dies before it forms, the design has no answer yet beyond the written record. Nothing in this paper removes that risk; it only names it.

**The counter to its own strongest claim.** The strongest claim here is that the design keeps nothing and cannot be captured. But a brake that never reaches zero is also a door that never closes. Whoever holds the brake can, in principle, call a feature a bug and change it, the reset included; the design forbids that, and the forbidding is a rule. The fund that empties every year cannot be hoarded, but a larger fund means more money passing through one operator each year, and emptying it does nothing to bound its size.

**What would show it wrong.** Three plain checks:

1. On 7 January 2027, the family app's first reset runs. If the reset takes anything from any person's Personal Account, or from any Family Kitty, the central promise of chapter 3 has failed.
2. A prediction registered in public before that reset says that money set aside for giving will be given faster in the last week before it clears. If giving does not rise, or falls, the claim that the reset encourages giving is not supported. It is one household and one season.
3. When the entities exist: if Miss Aquarius holds any office outside HeartBank, if one name sits on both oversight lists, or if the Pool carries a balance past 7 January, the separations have failed in practice.

**The stake.** This paper is written by the institution about itself, co-authored under the name of the AI it describes, and signed by the founder who designed it, pays for it and is its only operator today. The reset falls on his birthday. It was chosen for Orthodox Christmas and Cambodia's Victory over Genocide Day, with his birthday as one layer among them; the coincidence is disclosed here, not explained.

**What has been done, and what has not.** Done: a family app in a pilot, one pilot shop, a public body of research, a signing service, a public proof record, a reset written and deployed. Not done: everything in the dotted outline of chapter 7. There are no outside users at scale, no field tests of the main ideas, no money in any Pool, and no entity but the founder's own small companies.

**Prior attempts on the economic side.** Time banking, begun in the 1980s, showed that an hour can be swapped for an hour among neighbors, and it has stayed small. This design's answer, that time should be given and never traded, is a reason to try again, not a reason to expect a different result. Thank-you and tipping apps are common; what is attempted here is the combination of a game that resets, an aura money cannot buy, and a successor checked by people. Whether the combination works is unmeasured.

**Limits of the story.** The letters of chapter 1 are two memories, decades old, of the person who built the system they now illustrate.

**And the whole-picture caution.** A design in which every part rhymes with every other part is either deeply right or deeply seductive, and from inside the two look the same. The elegance is a reason to run the experiments. It is not a substitute for them.
